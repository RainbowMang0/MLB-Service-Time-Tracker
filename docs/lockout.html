What's being negotiated

The Basic Agreement is the contract between MLB's 30 clubs and the players' union (the MLBPA) that governs pay, free agency, arbitration, roster rules and more. Based on public reporting as talks continue, the clubs have proposed a salary cap paired with a minimum payroll floor, and the union has rejected a cap while pushing for a higher minimum salary and other player-side gains. Other reported topics include the length of the regular season and playoff format. None of this is final — it is what has been reported, not an agreement.

Lockout vs. strike, and the key dates

A lockout is management shutting the business down and barring players from club facilities; a strike is players refusing to work. The 2021-22 stoppage was a lockout: it began December 2, 2021 and ran 99 days, ending March 10, 2022. Opening Day moved from March 31 to April 7, but the sides later agreed to play a full 162-game season anyway, so no games were ultimately missed. The current agreement expires Dec. 1, 2026.

How a lockout affects service time, Super Two and arbitration

No service time accrues while the season is shut down — an offseason lockout itself doesn't cost a day, because service time is only earned during the championship season, and that includes off-days: a player is credited for every day on the active roster or injured list, not just days his team actually plays. What would matter is regular-season days actually missed; in 2021-22 the eventual deal restored a full 162-game season, so no player's service time or free-agency timeline ended up shorter for it — that is public history, not a guarantee about any future lockout. A lockout has historically pushed back offseason business broadly, including salary arbitration scheduling and tender deadlines, and it has no effect on the Super Two rule's mechanics themselves — only on how much of a season there ends up being to measure it against.

Minor league options, free agency and transactions

During a lockout, clubs cannot sign free agents, make trades, or complete any other transaction involving a player on the 40-man roster. Optioning a player is itself a 40-man roster move, so option moves freeze right along with everything else in that group; business involving players who are not on a 40-man roster — most of the minor leagues — continues as normal. FAQ

Does service time accrue during an MLB lockout?

Not during the offseason. Service time is only earned for days on a major league roster during the championship season, so an offseason lockout — one that starts in December, for example — doesn't cost a day on its own; there's no season running for the clock to measure. If a lockout ends up delaying the start of a season and regular-season days are actually lost, whether those days count toward service time depends on the settlement that ends the stoppage — it has been handled differently across past work stoppages.

What's the difference between a lockout and a strike?

A lockout is called by the owners, who shut business down and bar players from club facilities. A strike is called by the players, who refuse to work. The 2021-22 work stoppage was a lockout; earlier disputes in MLB history, including the 1994-95 stoppage, were players' strikes.

When does the current CBA expire, and when could a lockout start?

The agreement expires December 1, 2026, specifically 11:59 p.m. ET that night. In the last round of bargaining, owners locked players out within a day of the old deal expiring, so a similar timeline is widely expected again if no new agreement is reached first.

Are free-agent signings and trades frozen during a lockout?

Yes. Clubs can't sign players to major league contracts, trade players who are on a 40-man roster, or make other 40-man moves while a lockout is in effect — which is why that kind of business typically rushes to beat the deadline. Minor league contracts, which don't touch a 40-man roster, can continue.

Does a lockout affect salary arbitration or minor league options?

A lockout halts the business side of the sport broadly, which has historically pushed back tender deadlines and arbitration-related scheduling along with everything else. Options apply only to players on the 40-man roster, so option moves freeze right along with the other 40-man transactions above — the option rule itself doesn't change.

How long did the last MLB lockout last?

99 days: it began December 2, 2021 and ended March 10, 2022. Opening Day moved from March 31 to April 7, but the two sides agreed to play a full 162-game season on a compressed schedule, so no games were ultimately missed.
