Super Two: this winter's projected cutoff

Of 140 players in the live 2.000-3.000 year class: 21 project as Super Two, 6 are on the bubble, and 112 fall below the projected range.

A player with at least 2 but fewer than 3 years of major league service reaches arbitration a year early -- "Super Two" -- if he ranks in the top 22% of that class, by total service, and accrued at least 86 days of service in the preceding season. MLB does not publish the class or the cutoff; this project computes both from its own database of every tracked player's season-by-season service time, which is close to the league-wide population the rule is defined against.

After the 2026 season, the 2.000-3.000 year class had 136 players, and the top 30 of them (the top 22%) fell at or above 2.125 years of service (469 days) -- this project's own computed cutoff. Projected cutoff range: 2.125 to 2.132 years of service (469-476 days).

This is shown as a range, not a single number, because the computed figure is not exact. In the 5 seasons with a published figure to check against, this project's computed cutoff has missed it by 2 to 12 days. The high end is reported, unofficial: one day above Jordan Beck's service time. The live class below is measured against both ends of the range.

The cutoff is not fixed -- it falls wherever that year's class falls, and it has moved year to year historically. The figures above and below are this project's own estimate, not an official MLB/MLBPA number.
