Super Two: this winter's projected cutoff

Of 138 players in the live 2.000-3.000 year class: 24 project as Super Two, 0 are on the bubble, and 111 fall below the projected range.

A player with at least 2 but fewer than 3 years of major league service reaches arbitration a year early -- "Super Two" -- if he ranks in the top 22% of that class, by total service, and accrued at least 86 days of service in the preceding season. MLB does not publish the class or the cutoff; this project computes both from its own database of every tracked player's season-by-season service time, which is close to the league-wide population the rule is defined against.

After the 2026 season, 142 players had between 2.000 and 3.000 years and at least 86 days of service that season, and the top 31 of them (the top 22%) fell at or above 2.132 years of service (476 days). Projected cutoff: 2.132. That is the line this site currently projects the live class against, below.

The cutoff is not fixed -- it falls wherever that year's class falls, and it has moved year to year historically. The figures above and below are this project's own estimate, not an official MLB/MLBPA number.
