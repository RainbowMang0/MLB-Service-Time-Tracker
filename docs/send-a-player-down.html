Players/Can a team send a player down?

Can a team send a player down?

An option, a designation for assignment, and an outright assignment are three different ways a club takes a player off the active roster.

Option · DFA · Outright

Optional assignment

Yes, if he still has an option year. The club can send him to the minors and keep him on the 40-man roster. He leaves the active roster. He does not leave the organization.

Most players get 3 option years. One year is used when he spends 20 or more days in the minors on an optional assignment in that season. Several trips in the same season still count as one year. A stay shorter than 20 days does not use the year, and those days still count as major league service. How a day of service is counted is a separate question.

A player who has used those years is out of options. The club cannot send him down and keep the 40-man spot. A player with more than five years of major league service has to agree before the club can option him. Some players qualify for one more option year. This site does not guess which ones. Look up a player for the count this site does publish.

Example. A catcher on the 40-man has one option year left. The club can send him to Triple-A in the spring and call him back. If he is down for 20 days or more, that uses the last option year. If he is back sooner, it does not.

Designation for assignment

A DFA takes him off the 40-man roster. It is not a release. He is still under contract while the club decides the next move.

The club has 7 days to trade him, to put him on waivers, or to release him. If another club claims him, he joins that club's 40-man roster. If he clears waivers for an outright assignment, he can be sent to the minors and he comes off the 40-man. A release is its own move: the contract ends and he can sign anywhere. Designating him does not do that by itself.

Example. A reliever who is out of options is designated on a Monday. He is off the 40-man that day, and he is still that club's player. Within 7 days the club trades him, tries to send him to the minors through waivers, or releases him.

Outright assignment

An outright sends him to the minors and removes him from the 40-man, after he clears waivers. It is the path a club uses when he is out of options, or when a designation is not resolved by a trade or a claim.

A player with more than three years of major league service, or who has been outrighted before, can turn the assignment down and become a free agent. A player with more than five years of service can refuse to be sent to the minors at all. What a year of service is lives on the service-time page. Who is arbitration-eligible is a related list, not the same test.

If he accepts, or if he cannot refuse, the contract continues. He plays in the minors and he is no longer on the 40-man. If he turns it down and becomes a free agent, that club no longer controls the contract.

Example. A first baseman with four years of service clears waivers. He can turn down the outright and become a free agent. A teammate with one year of service who has never been outrighted has to accept. He stays under contract, in the minors, off the 40-man.

The 40-man roster and the active roster

The active roster is who can play in a game. During the season it holds 26 players, and 28 in September. The 40-man roster is the reserve list. It holds 40 players: the active roster, players on the injured list, and some players in the minors.

A player has to be on the 40-man before he can be on the active roster. An option takes him off the active roster and leaves him on the 40-man. A designation or an outright takes him off the 40-man. The injured list is not a trip to the minors. He is still on the 40-man.

Why being out of options matters

While option years remain, a club can move a player between the majors and the minors and keep the roster spot. Once he is out of options, that door is shut. Sending him to the minors means taking him off the 40-man, which means a designation and waivers. Another club can claim him. That is why an out-of-options player is harder to move down, in spring training and again around roster deadlines.

The out-of-options list is this site's own count from public data. It is not the league's official record.

Rule 5 protection

November 17, 2026 is the day clubs set the reserve list. A player on the 40-man roster that day is protected from the Rule 5 draft. The draft itself is a later event, scheduled for December 9, 2026.

In general, a player who is not already on the 40-man has to be added by that day if another club would be allowed to select him. A player signed at 18 or younger is generally eligible after 5 seasons. A player signed at 19 or older is generally eligible after 4 seasons. Clubs protect the ones they do not want selected by placing them on the reserve list. This page does not decide who is eligible.

The non-tender deadline

November 20, 2026 is the non-tender deadline. A club that does not offer next season's contract to a player on the 40-man who is not yet a free agent makes him a free agent. That is not an option, a designation, or an outright. It is a decision about next year's contract.

Common questions

Can a team send a player down?

Yes, when he is on the 40-man roster and still has an option year. Most players get 3 option years. The club can send him to the minors and keep him on the 40-man. Once those years are used, he is out of options, and the club has to designate him and pass him through waivers first.

What does out of options mean?

It means his option years are used up. The club cannot send him to the minors and leave him on the 40-man roster. A short stay of fewer than 20 days in one season does not use an option year.

What does DFA mean?

DFA means designated for assignment. The player comes off the 40-man roster immediately. The club has 7 days to trade him, put him on waivers, or release him. He is still under contract during that window.

Is a DFA the same as a release?

No. A release ends the club's contract and the player can sign anywhere. A designation only starts the 7-day window. The club might still trade him or send him to the minors.

What is an outright assignment?

It takes a player off the 40-man roster and assigns him to the minors after he clears waivers. A player with more than three years of major league service, or who has been outrighted before, can turn it down and become a free agent. If he accepts, the contract continues and he plays in the minors.

What is the difference between the 40-man roster and the active roster?

The active roster is the 26 players who can play in a game. The 40-man roster is the reserve list. It includes the active roster, players on the injured list, and some players in the minors.

When is the 2026 non-tender deadline?

November 20, 2026. A club that does not offer next season's contract to a player on the 40-man who is not yet a free agent makes him a free agent. That is a different move from an option, a designation, or an outright.
